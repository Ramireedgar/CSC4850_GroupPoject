Evaluation Metrics Explanation

When predicting soccer match outcomes (Home Win, Draw, Away Win), simple accuracy doesn't always tell the whole story, especially since match outcomes can be imbalanced (e.g., home wins happen more often than draws). Because this is a multi-class problem, we evaluate our models using weighted averages for precision, recall, and F1-score to get a more nuanced understanding of their performance.

1. Accuracy
- What it is: The percentage of matches where the model correctly predicted the outcome (Home Win, Draw, Away Win).
- In this project: An accuracy of 50% means the model guesses the correct match result 1 out of 2 times. While it seems low at a glance, predicting soccer is notoriously chaotic and difficult. A baseline random guess would only be around 33%. Accuracy is a great high-level indicator but can be misleading if the dataset strongly favors one outcome (like Home Wins).

2. Precision
- What it is: Out of all the matches the model predicted as a specific outcome (e.g., Draw), how many were actually that outcome? (True Positives / (True Positives + False Positives))
- In this project: If a model has high precision for 'Draws', it means when it predicts a draw, you can be highly confident it will actually be a draw. A low precision means the model "cries wolf" too often—predicting an outcome when it shouldn't, leading to false alarms.

3. Recall (Sensitivity)
- What it is: Out of all the actual matches that ended in a specific outcome (e.g., Draw), how many did the model successfully find and predict? (True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives))
- In this project: If the recall for 'Away Wins' is high, it means the model is great at catching upsets or away victories. A low recall means the model is missing these outcomes entirely, perhaps misclassifying them safely as home wins instead.

4. F1-Score
- What it is: The harmonic mean of Precision and Recall. It mathematically balances the two metrics into a single number.
- In this project: The F1-score is incredibly important because predicting draws is generally much harder than predicting home wins. A model might have decent accuracy by simply guessing "Home Win" almost every single time, but its F1-score would be low because its precision and recall on draws and away wins would be terrible. A high F1-score indicates a robust, well-rounded model that performs fairly and accurately across all three possible outcomes.
